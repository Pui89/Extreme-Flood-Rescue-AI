# Free-First Marketing, SEO and Partnership Playbook

## Purpose
Explain the Extreme Flood Rescue AI concept to emergency-management, humanitarian, robotics, and research partners without implying operational rescue readiness.

## Positioning
**A simulation-first AI and robotics concept for flood-response support across ground, surface-water, and aerial platforms.**

State clearly:
- Simulation and software evidence are not proof of safe real-world rescue performance.
- Water depths of 1–6 m are scenario targets, not verified operating limits.
- Do not imply that a robot can reliably detect, reach, or rescue people in dangerous water without validated testing and qualified oversight.
- Human responders remain responsible for emergency decisions.

## Free-first channel plan
1. **Public project page:** show the problem, proposed multi-platform workflow, status, safety boundaries, and partner contact route.
2. **Demonstration:** show simulation or recorded software behaviour with a persistent “simulation/concept” label. Do not use staged visuals as evidence of a real rescue.
3. **Search visibility:** use plain-language headings, descriptive image alt text, accessible page structure, and natural terms such as flood-response robotics, search-and-rescue robotics, and disaster-response AI.
4. **Evidence notes:** publish reproducible simulation scenarios, assumptions, failure cases, and metrics when measured. Distinguish simulated results from field trials.
5. **Partner discovery:** contact universities, robotics clubs, disaster-response organisations, and public-safety experts with a short, respectful request for feedback—not a claim that the system is ready to deploy.
6. **User feedback:** ask what information and safety controls responders would require before evaluating a prototype.

## Suggested page structure
- Problem and intended users.
- Concept diagram: sensing → mapping/scene assessment → candidate route or assistance plan → human review.
- Roles of ground robot, boat, and drone, with each labelled as proposed unless implemented and tested.
- Simulation scenarios and documented assumptions.
- Safety, privacy, communications-loss, and human-override considerations.
- Current status and explicit non-operational warning.
- Contact invitation for technical review or simulation collaboration.

## SEO and trust checklist
- Use descriptive page titles and concise summaries.
- Label synthetic data, simulated scenes, and conceptual images.
- Do not publish identifiable victim imagery or sensitive incident data without a lawful basis and permission.
- Avoid sensational claims, fake testimonials, fabricated deployments, and unverified statistics.
- Audit the public page with a free scanner such as FreeScan.app, then manually verify findings.

## Partnership questions
1. Which flood-response decision or information gap matters most?
2. What environmental conditions and failure modes should a simulation cover?
3. What evidence would be required before a controlled field test?
4. What safety, communications, and human-override rules are mandatory?
5. Who can review the concept without exposing sensitive operational data?

## Free-first tools and cautions
- **FreeScan.app:** optional initial public-page audit; monitoring upgrades are not required.
- **LiveDemo:** optional free tier for a small number of published walkthroughs; watermark and limits apply. A simple recorded simulation is sufficient.
- **Paid ads, lead scraping, and automated outreach:** avoid at this stage. Trust and qualified expert feedback matter more than traffic volume.
- Do not add paid APIs, cloud GPU, hosting, or external accounts without explicit approval.

## Definition of done
- Every demo distinguishes simulation, prototype, and field-tested capability.
- Safety boundaries and human oversight are visible.
- No private incident data or unlicensed imagery is exposed.
- Claims link to reproducible evidence or are explicitly marked as planned.
- The contact route works and no paid service is enabled without approval.
